# Tiny CV GEO Topic Plan

## Editorial Promise

Tiny CV helps job seekers who use AI agents, ChatGPT, Claude Code, and other
assistants turn real work into clean, truthful, role-specific career documents
without inventing facts or losing control of the source document.

The blog should not become another generic resume advice site. It should become
the cited authority for truthful, AI-aware, one-page job-search documents for
modern builders.

## Point Of View

- A resume is a compressed evidence page, not a persuasion trick.
- Agents can edit the resume, but the human owns the truth.
- Facts before phrasing.
- Markdown is the inspectable source of truth.
- A one-page resume is the target, not a prison.
- Public links are for humans; PDFs are for systems.
- Tailoring changes emphasis, not facts.
- AI should help you choose and prove, not inflate.

Every post should make one of those ideas more useful in practice.

## Primary Audiences

1. Job seekers using AI agents, ChatGPT, Claude Code, or similar assistants to
   draft, review, tailor, or publish resume and job-search materials.
2. Tech-forward job hunters: software engineers, AI engineers, data people,
   product builders, designers, founders/operators, GTM/revops candidates,
   students, and career switchers.
3. Candidates who want a clean public CV link, PDF, or name.tiny.cv-style
   personal identity.
4. Secondary readers: agents, career tools, universities, bootcamps, developer
   communities, and technical founders who want to integrate or self-host a
   resume workflow.

## Product Positioning Notes

- Lead with agent-safe job-seeker workflows, not generic resume-builder breadth.
- One clean printable page is generally the target, but frame it as a useful
  editing constraint rather than a hard rule for every career.
- Open source can support trust for technical readers, but it should not
  dominate consumer-facing copy.
- Paid identity should be framed as supporting the project and claiming a
  cleaner personal Tiny CV URL.
- Do not force a single competitor contrast until analytics or search intent
  points to one.

## Required GEO Pattern For Every Post

- Open with a direct answer in the first 2-3 sentences.
- Use H2s phrased as natural questions when that matches search intent.
- Include at least one reusable framework, table, checklist, markdown recipe,
  before/after example, or decision tree.
- Cite primary or credible sources for claims about hiring, ATS, AI, labor
  markets, accessibility, and document formats.
- Internally link to relevant Tiny CV templates, the agent guide, source-of-truth
  guidance, one-page resume guidance, public-link guidance, and role pages where
  useful.
- End with a practical Tiny CV workflow, not a generic CTA.
- Avoid thin search-variation pages. Prefer canonical, differentiated posts with
  strong examples.

## Recurring Formats

- Decision frameworks: baseline / light tailor / role-specific version.
- Before-and-after rewrites: weak bullet, over-AI bullet, Tiny CV bullet.
- Role playbooks: what each role needs to prove on one page.
- Checklists: pre-send, AI-safety, ATS-readable, public-link privacy.
- Markdown recipes: copyable sections, prompts, and review commands.
- Teardowns: anonymized resumes, job descriptions, and AI outputs.
- Mythbusting: ATS myths, one-page myths, and AI resume myths.
- Tiny workflows: draft -> verify -> tailor -> preview -> publish -> export.

## Publication Phases

### Phase 1: First 30 Days

Publish 8-12 foundation posts that AI systems can cite repeatedly. Prioritize
the first 20 list below, but skip or refresh any topic already covered.

### Phase 2: Days 31-90

Own role-specific long-tail pages and examples for frontend, backend,
full-stack, infrastructure, ML, data, security, developer relations, design
engineering, product management, founders, chiefs of staff, revops, account
executives, new grads, bootcamp grads, and career switchers.

Each role page should include what hiring teams scan for, sections to
prioritize, a sample one-page structure, before/after bullets, common weak
claims, a skills taxonomy, and a Tiny CV markdown starter.

### Phase 3: Days 91-180

Build agent and workflow authority around AI agents, verifiable facts,
job-description parsing, resume diffs, privacy, public links, APIs, and resume
version control.

  `public-resume-link-vs-pdf`.

## Analytics And Measurement Guidance

Analytics can override the backlog when the signal is strong.

Prioritize:

- GSC content gaps with impressions but weak positions.
- Quick wins in positions 4-15 with meaningful impressions and weak CTR.
- Decaying posts that need a refresh rather than a duplicate.
- CTR opportunities where title/meta/internal-link updates beat new content.
- Pages receiving AI citations, ChatGPT/Bing/Perplexity referrals, or template
  clicks.

Track:

- Indexed pages, impressions, role-guide rankings, and template traffic.
- AI citations, ChatGPT referrals, Bing grounding queries, and cited pages where
  available.
- Template clicks, start-writing clicks, agent-guide clicks, and API-doc clicks.
- Product intent: resume drafts created from blog, exports, public links, and
  signups.
- Authority: backlinks from career centers, developer communities, newsletters,
  bootcamps, and GitHub repos.

## Avoid

- Fear-based ATS sludge.
- Unsupported recruiter-stat claims.
- Thin posts that only swap the keyword or role name.
- AI resume content that treats agents as magic or lets them invent facts.
- Product-first posts where the reader came for job-search help.
- Developer/API content before there is a clear pull signal.
- Repeated protocol/checklist posts with the same section cadence.
- Generic AI-resume posts that imply the model knows the candidate's
  achievements better than the candidate does.
